## Phase Boundary

Deliver real Bluesky propagation data end to end, inside stated bounds and with honest edge semantics. In order: a source spike (written decision, measured yield and a recorded real-data fixture), then a Postgres schema with Alembic migrations, a bounded Jetstream collector (idempotent, checkpoint-after-commit, deletes honoured, accounts pseudonymised), a deterministic enrich step (parents resolved, cascades rolled up, orphans explicit), a typed `/v1` read API with provenance, and database-served news context refreshed by a bounded job. No fabricated user or event is ever on the live path.

Requirements: DATA-01, DATA-02, DATA-03, DATA-04, DATA-05, DATA-06. ROADMAP success criteria 1-5. FND-02's Bluesky and database failure paths are verified here (SC5).

**Not in this phase:** any classifier output (no `risk`, no `claim_scores` in any `/v1` response; Phase 4), the dashboard (Phase 5), deployment, scheduled workflows and the managed Postgres (Phase 6), Mastodon, Jetstream Replay or Snapshot history and AppView backfill (v2), cascade features and the simulator (v2; the schema still keeps `observed_at` and `occurred_at` apart so `features(events, t0, horizon)` stays possible), and any Bluesky credential. The Phase 1 error/provenance contract (RFC 9457 problem+json with `synthetic`, `source_status`, `data_as_of`; 502 for a bad answer, 503 with `Retry-After` for unreachable) and the Phase 2 `/health`, `/ready`, `/v1`, CORS, rate-limit, cache and retry decisions are frozen. ### OWNER DECISIONS (framed, not decided; ratified after the spike)

- **OD-1 (OWNER DECISION) Retention policy.** Question: what is stored, for how long, and what is removed when.
  - Options. **RT-1 Minimal:** store no post text at all; the claim text of a root is the publisher's link-card title; structure kept 30 days then deleted. **RT-2 Short-TTL text (recommended):** post text of a root is kept 14 days from the root's `observed_at` and then NULLed; link-card title, canonical URL, structure and counts are kept 90 days; the whole cascade is then hard-deleted; non-root events never hold text; tombstones are purged 7 days after the delete. **RT-3 Demo lifetime:** root text kept for the life of the cascade (up to the global cap).
  - Trade-offs. RT-1 has the least privacy exposure and the smallest database and fits a headline-trained classifier, but reviewers see no author wording and Phase 4 cannot score commentary. RT-2 gives reviewers wording during the triage window and bounds exposure, including deletes missed during an ingest gap (a missed delete lives at most 14 days), at the cost of a purge job and a 14-day window in which a post can be re-found by text search, which weakens the account pseudonymisation of D-17 for roots. RT-3 gives the richest demo and the weakest privacy and ToS position.
  - Facts the owner needs. Text volume is not the binding storage constraint (a root's text is at most 300 graphemes, about 1 KB; the events table dominates), privacy and terms are. Bluesky requires deleting content on a user's request; the terms contain no research clause. Public posts are still personal data; deletion propagation, minimisation and a purge job are the defence. An operator-run erase command by DID (computes the pseudonym with the secret and tombstones everything) is a small optional addition for a missed delete. The demo GIF and README screenshots use pseudonymised data under every option.
  - Recommendation: RT-2 with 14 / 90 / 7 days, PROVISIONAL. Spike measurements that settle it: S-5 (roots per day, share with commentary text), S-8 (bytes per root and runway), S-7 (time-to-delete: if most root deletions happen within hours the short TTL covers them), S-10 (terms wording).
  - Plans consume: `RETENTION_TEXT_DAYS`, `RETENTION_CASCADE_DAYS`, `RETENTION_TOMBSTONE_DAYS`; RT-1 is `RETENTION_TEXT_DAYS=0` with text never written.
- **OD-2 (OWNER DECISION) How provisional and duplicate cascades merge.** Question: when evidence shows two cascades overlap, do they merge.
  - Situations. (A) Re-rooting: events stored under cascade X are later shown to hang below another tracked cascade Y. With edges stored as record facts (D-12) and roots limited to top-level, non-reply, non-quote posts, this cannot happen by construction under allowlist seeding; it can only appear if a policy promotes a reply or quote to its own root (OD-3 option C). (B) Claim-level duplicates: several independent roots link the same article. (C) Stream-order inversion where a child arrives before its root: handled by D-14 (dropped, measured by S-4), not by merging.
  - Options. **M0 Never merge:** a cascade's identity is fixed when its root is first stored; overlap is recorded as a relation and shown ("also appears in cascade Z"). **M1 Never merge, group by claim (recommended):** M0 plus `cascades.claim_id` so the API can filter and summarise all cascades that share a canonical article URL (`GET /v1/cascades?claim_id=`). **M2 Derived full merge:** enrich derives membership from the parent graph and absorbs cascade X into Y (`absorbed_by_cascade_id`, old id redirects), giving one tree per story.
  - Trade-offs. M0 and M1 are order-independent by construction, keep cascade ids and "observed since" stable, and need no collector code; they leave duplicates visible. M2 gives one canonical tree but churns ids and permalinks, changes orphan and "observed since" semantics retroactively, adds a merge path that needs its own determinism tests, and buys nothing under allowlist seeding.
  - Facts the owner needs. OD-2 is conditional on OD-3: with option A (allowlist) only situation (B) exists and M1 settles it at no cost; with option C situation (A) appears and M2 becomes worth considering. The UI feed (Phase 5) may want duplicates grouped; M1 gives it the key.
  - Recommendation: M1, PROVISIONAL. Settled by S-9 (rate of A and B) together with the OD-3 choice: if situation A is under 1% of cascades and B is a visible share of the feed, ratify M1; if the owner picks OD-3 option C, revisit.
  - Plans consume: `CASCADE_MERGE_POLICY` in {`none`, `claim_group`, `derived`}; only `claim_group` is built unless the owner ratifies otherwise.
- **OD-3 (OWNER DECISION) Tracked-root selection policy and its bounds.** Question: which top-level posts become tracked roots (everything else is dropped at the adapter boundary), and how much may be stored.
  - Options. **A Allowlist seed (recommended):** a top-level post (not a reply, not a quote) becomes a root when its external-embed or link-facet URL has a canonical host on a committed, reviewable allowlist of English-language news organisations and fact-checkers (at most 60 domains, each entry commented with its category), and passes D-13's hygiene filters. **B Claim-URL match (additive):** also seed when the canonical URL equals the URL of a stored news headline from the DATA-06 job; precise, cheap, low yield, ties cascades to news context. **C Engagement promotion:** keep a bounded in-memory counter of reposts, replies and quotes per untracked subject and promote a post to root when it crosses a threshold within a window; finds viral content of any topic, but it buffers every untracked repost reference, stores non-news content (noise, adult, abusive), loses events before promotion, and creates the re-rooting situation of OD-2. **D Authenticated search seed:** seed from `searchPosts` for the news-job topics with an app password; needs a credential and session handling, the public host load-sheds that endpoint with 403 and the workaround is forbidden, and backfilled events would carry fetch time instead of stream time, which breaks D-08. D is rejected for v1.
  - Bounds (apply to every option, PROVISIONAL): at most 500 active roots, a root tracks for 48 hours of stream time after its own `observed_at`, at most 5,000 stored events per cascade (events beyond the cap are counted in `overflow_count`, not stored), at most 300,000 stored events in total, English only, adult self-labelled posts excluded. When a cap is reached the collector stops promoting or storing, counts the refusals in the run summary and never evicts.
  - Trade-offs and facts. The allowlist is an editorial choice and a selection bias that must be disclosed in the dashboard and the README limitations (DOC-02); choose entries by category rules (wire services, public broadcasters, IFCN-listed fact-checkers), not political lean, and publish the list with the spike document. Yield is unknown until S-5; the heavy tail (a few viral cascades hold most events) is why the per-cascade cap exists. Option A needs no credential, stores only link-bearing news posts, and has the smallest privacy footprint.
  - Recommendation: A, with B enabled only if S-5 shows A's yield is too low to populate a feed; C and D deferred. PROVISIONAL, ratified after S-5 and S-9.
  - Plans consume: `TRACK_ALLOWLIST_FILE`, `TRACK_MAX_ACTIVE_ROOTS`, `TRACK_ROOT_TTL_HOURS`, `TRACK_MAX_EVENTS_PER_CASCADE`, `STORE_MAX_EVENTS_TOTAL`, `TRACK_REQUIRE_LANG`, `TRACK_SEED_CLAIM_URL_MATCH` (boolean for B).

### Source, protocol and collector
- **D-05:** Instances. Target Jetstream v2 on the public Bluesky hosts: primary `wss://jetstream.us-east.bsky.network/xrpc/network.bsky.jetstream.subscribeEvents`, failover `jetstream.us-west.bsky.network`, subprotocol `xrpc.v1.json`. The only stream verified from this container is the v1 host `jetstream2.us-east`, so the spike must stream from at least one v2 host through the container proxy before this decision stands (S-1). If no v2 host streams from here, the collector uses the v1 dialect and the decision document and README say so. All generation-specific parts (URL and parameters, subprotocol, envelope unwrapping, time field `time` versus `time_us`, cursor field, `kinds` support) live in one dialect module; one real recorded frame per dialect is the contract-test input; everything downstream sees only `NormalizedEvent`. Failover across instances resumes by microsecond-timestamp cursor, never by a per-instance `seq`. Not used: community-run instances (unvetted operators) and a self-hosted Jetstream (it archives the whole network). — **Reversibility:** reversible — one module.
- **D-06:** Subscription. Collections `app.bsky.feed.post` and `app.bsky.feed.repost`, named explicitly with no wildcard; no likes (about an order of magnitude more events and no cascade edge), no graph or profile collections, no `dids` filter. On v2, `kinds=commit`, `kinds=account` and `kinds=sync`: account and sync events are needed to honour account deletion (D-16) and arrive regardless of the collection filter, so each is hashed and dropped client-side unless its pseudonym is already in `accounts`; `identity` is not requested (handles are not stored). Compression stays off in the first version: Jetstream's zstd mode needs a dictionary file (a new vendored artifact) for a stream that measured about 77 events/s, and S-2 records bytes/s so the choice can be revisited with evidence. The WebSocket reader has a bounded `max_size` (2 MiB); a frame that is not a JSON object, exceeds the bound, or fails shape validation is counted by reason and dropped. — **Reversibility:** reversible.
- **D-07:** Credentials and identification. The live tail needs no key, so the collector uses no Bluesky credential; `BLUESKY_HANDLE` and `BLUESKY_APP_PASSWORD` stay unused and unset in this phase. The User-Agent is `TruthTrail/<version>` with the repository URL and an optional operator contact from `COLLECTOR_CONTACT` (read from the environment, never committed). TLS verification is always on; the proxy and CA bundle come from the standard `HTTPS_PROXY` and `SSL_CERT_FILE` environment (the adapter accepts an explicit proxy argument for the container). HTTP 403 or 401 on the upgrade is fatal with a clear message and no retry.
- **D-08:** Time semantics. `observed_at` is the stream's own event time (v2 `time`, v1 `time_us`), converted to UTC `timestamptz` with microsecond precision. It is never the collector's wall clock, so a replay or a shuffled arrival yields identical values; ordering, velocity, tracking windows and the child-not-before-parent invariant use it. `occurred_at` is the record's author-asserted `createdAt`: untrusted, stored as given when parseable and within 1970 to now plus 1 year, otherwise NULL and counted, and never used for ordering. Order violations on real data (a child observed before its parent) are a counted data-quality metric (`order_violation_count`), never a database constraint and never "fixed" by editing a timestamp. Because the Phase 2 `validate_cascade` raises on the first violation (right for fixtures), Phase 3 adds a non-raising `find_order_violations()` beside it in `core/cascade.py`; existing signatures do not change.
- **D-09:** Reconnect, cursor rewind, gaps, checkpoint-after-commit. One database transaction per batch writes the upserts, the tombstones and the `ingest_checkpoints` row (cursor time in microseconds and, when present, `seq`), so a crash after commit loses nothing and a crash before commit repeats the batch harmlessly. Resume connects with `cursor = last_committed_time_us - INGEST_REWIND_SECONDS` (default 30 s, to be at least twice the largest stream-time inversion measured in S-4); the rewind re-delivers events that idempotency (D-15) absorbs. Reconnect uses exponential backoff with full jitter from 1 s to 60 s, gives up after 8 consecutive failures or the run's deadline, and treats 60 s of silence as a dead connection. A checkpoint older than `INGEST_MAX_RESUME_AGE_HOURS` (default 30, under the 36 h lookback) or a connect refused with HTTP 400 and a new floor starts at the tip or the floor and records an explicit gap in `ingest_runs` (`resume_gap`, from and to stream times); the API exposes gap counts so "observed since" never implies continuity. A delete is never lost to a rewind and a tombstone is never resurrected by a redelivered create (D-15, D-16).
- **D-10:** Run model. The collector is a bounded batch job, not an always-on service and never part of the API process: `python -m truthtrail.ingest run` stops at the first of `--max-seconds` (default 300), `--max-events` frames processed (default 500,000) and `--max-stored` rows written (default 20,000), and when `STORE_MAX_EVENTS_TOTAL` or the database budget (D-21) is reached. A run connects with the checkpoint cursor so the 36-hour lookback lets a scheduled job catch up on what it missed (S-3 measures the rate and proves it works without a key; if catch-up is unavailable the design degrades to sampling windows and the decision document must say so, because completeness and "observed since" then change). The batch pipeline is collect, then enrich (D-22), then purge, each idempotent and callable alone; Phase 3 ships the commands, Phase 6 schedules them (GitHub Actions cron against Neon). Exit code 0 means every normal stop including a reached bound; non-zero means configuration error, source failure after the reconnect budget, or database failure, and the run row records `status` and error class only. Graceful stop relies on the bounds and `KeyboardInterrupt`, not POSIX signals, so it works on Windows.
- **D-11:** Source adapters. `EventSource` yields `(NormalizedEvent, cursor)`; `JetstreamSource` owns connect, reconnect and backoff; `ReplaySource` reads the recorded fixture through the same normaliser and writer. `source_mode` is `live` or `replay` and is surfaced by `/v1/stats`; replayed data is real but not live and is never called synthetic. Tests never reach the real Jetstream: adapter tests run against a local `websockets` server on loopback that replays recorded frames, drops connections and refuses cursors.

### Edge semantics, orphans, idempotency, deletes
- **D-12:** Edge semantics. `edge_kind` is `root`, `reply`, `quote` or `repost`, a fact of the record, independent of what has been stored. Reply: `parent_uri` is `reply.parent.uri` and `reply_root_uri` is `reply.root.uri`; observed edge, `inferred = false`. Quote: `parent_uri` is the quoted record (`embed.record.uri`, or `embed.record.record.uri` for `recordWithMedia`); observed edge, `inferred = false`. A post that is both reply and quote is a reply and keeps the quote target in `quoted_uri`, a secondary relation that is never an edge (S-6 counts these). Repost: `parent_uri` is `subject.uri`, the post named by the record (the root in the common case); the repost belongs to the cascade of its subject; `inferred = true`; it has no depth, contributes to no chain and no `max_depth`, and is reported as a separate count. Reposts are never attached to another repost and never drawn as a reshare tree: the API returns them as a fan-out hanging off their subject with `inferred: true`, and no field anywhere implies who saw the post from whom. A root is a top-level, non-reply, non-quote post that passed selection. `depth` is the distance from the root along resolved reply and quote edges only. The database enforces `(edge_kind = 'root') = (parent_uri IS NULL)` and `inferred = (edge_kind = 'repost')`. — **Reversibility:** costly — columns, API fields and the Phase 5 legend depend on it.
- **D-13:** Seed eligibility mechanics (common to all OD-3 options). Link sources are `app.bsky.embed.external.uri` and link facets (`app.bsky.richtext.facet#link`) in the post text; S-6 compares them. URLs are canonicalised (lowercase host, strip `www.`, drop fragments and tracking parameters such as `utm_*`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, normalise the trailing slash) and are never fetched, followed or resolved by the collector (no SSRF surface, no redirect handling); shorteners are not on the allowlist, so they never seed, and are counted. Eligibility also requires `en` in the record's `langs` (absent counts as ineligible) and no adult self-label (`porn`, `sexual`, `nudity`, `graphic-media`). Every refusal is counted by reason in the run summary.
- **D-14:** Orphans are an explicit stored state and parents are never invented. `events.parent_state` is `none` (root), `resolved`, `orphan` (a non-root whose `parent_uri` does not match a stored row) or `invalid` (self-parent or cycle, quarantined and counted, never fatal). An orphan keeps its cascade through `reply_root_uri` when that root is stored; it is never re-parented to the root, and the tree endpoint returns it as a detached subtree. A child is stored only when its root or subject is already stored (the tracked set is the set of stored events of active cascades), so a child that arrives before its root is dropped and counted; no pending buffer in v1, and S-4 reports the rate so this can be revisited if it exceeds 1%. Orphans arise from a reply whose parent was never seen, a deleted and purged parent, or a gap; enrich flips an orphan to `resolved` if its parent is later stored. `orphan_count` counts direct orphans; `disconnected_count` counts every event not connected to its root through resolved edges (orphans and their descendants); both are exposed.
- **D-15:** Idempotency. The key is the event's `at://` URI in its pseudonymous form (D-17), unique in `events.uri`. Writes are `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (uri) DO NOTHING`: an observation is immutable, a replay never changes an existing row (a tombstone included), and an `update` operation on a post or repost is ignored and counted. `accounts` are upserted by pseudonym in the same statement batch. This is the reading of DATA-02's `parent_uri` and DATA-03's "keyed on the `at://` URI": the column names stay `uri` and `parent_uri`, and their values are the keyed forms of the `at://` URIs (D-17); the plan summaries state this reading for the verifier. The acceptance test replays the same input twice and compares row counts per table, and compares a canonical projection of the rows by natural key.
- **D-16:** Deletes. A delete commit on `app.bsky.feed.post` or `app.bsky.feed.repost`, an `account` event with `status` `deleted` or `takendown`, and a `sync` divergence marker (the Jetstream documentation says it removes all of that account's records) are all handled, always by the same rule: tombstone. A tombstone keeps `uri`, `edge_kind`, `parent_uri`, `reply_root_uri`, `cascade_id`, `observed_at` and `deleted_at` so the cascade skeleton stays connected, and removes `account_id`, `occurred_at`, `text`, `claim_id` and `lang`; a deleted repost stops counting; a deleted root marks `cascades.root_deleted_at`, unlinks its claim (the claim row holds publisher text and is shared by every cascade that links the same article, so it is removed by the purge job only when nothing references it), and hides the cascade from the feed by default; the purge job hard-deletes tombstones `RETENTION_TOMBSTONE_DAYS` later and the affected children then become honest orphans. Delete lookups run in batch by key against stored events, including events of cascades whose tracking window has ended, because anything stored must stay deletable. An account-wide rule resolves the pseudonym with the secret and tombstones every row of that account. `deactivated`, `suspended` and `desynchronized` are not deletions and are ignored. Because tombstones are kept as rows, a redelivered create cannot resurrect content. The proof (SC3): a constructed delete frame (D-04) turns a stored row into a tombstone, and a replay of the original create does not bring the content back.

### Privacy
- **D-17:** Accounts are pseudonymised, and the stored identifiers are keyed hashes, not raw DIDs or URIs.
  - What is stored: `accounts.pseudonym` = lowercase base32 of `HMAC-SHA256(PSEUDONYM_SECRET, "acct\0" + did)` truncated to 16 characters (80 bits); every `uri`, `parent_uri`, `reply_root_uri`, `quoted_uri` and `root_uri` column holds the URI key = lowercase base32 of `HMAC-SHA256(PSEUDONYM_SECRET, "uri\0" + canonical at:// URI)` truncated to 20 characters (100 bits) (domain-separated prefixes so the two spaces never collide); no DID, no handle, no follower count, no display name, no avatar, and no clear `rkey` is stored. The API shows at most the first 8 characters of a pseudonym.
  - Why a keyed hash and not the raw DID: an `at://` URI contains the author's DID, so a raw URI column would defeat account pseudonymisation; a DID is a stable global identifier that joins to a person's handle and full public history, which would make the database a ready-made index of who engaged with which claim, a profiling asset beyond this tool's purpose (the tool analyses claims, not people); and no feature needs the raw value, because unique-account counts, parent resolution and delete matching all work on a deterministic key.
  - Why HMAC with a server-side secret and not a plain or fixed-salt hash: the DID space is enumerable from the public directory, so an unkeyed or publicly salted hash is reversible by a dictionary attack; a random per-row salt would break the equality joins the cascade needs; only a single secret key keeps the joins and resists the dictionary.
  - What we give up: no "open this post on Bluesky" link in v1 (the claim is the linked article, which the UI shows), and the secret is long-lived, so rotating it re-keys every identifier and is a rebuild.
  - Honesty limits, to be stated in DOC-02: this is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation; with stored timestamps and (for the retention window) root text, a party holding a network archive can still re-link a record, and a leaked secret plus a leaked database re-enables dictionary reversal.
  - `PSEUDONYM_SECRET` (at least 32 random bytes) is read from the environment only by processes that ingest or erase (collector, jobs); the API process never needs it, which is least privilege; those processes refuse to start without it, error text never contains it, and it is a `SecretStr`. Fixtures use a clearly fake test secret. — **Reversibility:** one-way — raw identifiers are never stored, so past data cannot be recovered in clear, and a scheme or key change re-keys every row; the owner's ratification of the spike document is the checkpoint for this item.
- **D-18:** Data minimisation. Stored per event: the keys and edge fields above, `observed_at`, `occurred_at`, `lang` (roots), `account_id`. Post text exists only on root events, only under the ratified OD-1, and is NULL with `text_purged_at` once expired; reply, quote and repost events never hold text (no v1 feature needs it); the root's claim row holds the canonical link URL, its host and the publisher's link-card title (publisher text, not personal), kept for the cascade's life. News headlines are headline, URL, source name and times only; no article body. Raw frames, raw DIDs, handles and post text never appear in logs, error bodies, metrics, fixtures or the repository; log lines carry counts, error class names and pseudonym prefixes of at most 8 characters.

### Storage and access layer
- **D-19:** Access layer: SQLAlchemy 2.x (STACK.md pins 2.1.4; the plan resolves and exact-pins on PyPI as Phase 2's D-24 did), `psycopg[binary,pool]` 3.3.x as the only driver, Alembic 1.20.x, all synchronous.
  - Rationale: the Phase 2 service is sync (`def` handlers in the threadpool, sync readiness callables, slowapi on sync routes), Alembic is sync, tests need no async plugin, and the collector is async only at the socket edge (it hands each batch to a worker thread with `asyncio.to_thread`, which also applies natural backpressure); STACK.md names this as the allowed alternative and warns against mixing styles.
  - ORM declarative models (`Mapped[...]`, one `MetaData` with a constraint naming convention) are the schema's single source of truth and `alembic check` proves models and migrations agree; hot paths (batch upsert, delete, enrich write-back) use Core statements. All SQL is parameterised; no f-string SQL (an AST test enforces it).
  - Migrations live inside the package (`src/truthtrail/db/migrations/`, with a root `alembic.ini` that points at them) so the image carries them and the readiness check can read the head revision; revisions are reviewed by hand and CI runs upgrade, downgrade and upgrade against a real Postgres (SC2).
  - `DATABASE_URL` accepts `postgresql://`, `postgres://` or `postgresql+psycopg://` and is normalised to the psycopg 3 dialect in one function; URLs are `SecretStr` and never echoed.
  - Engine: `pool_pre_ping`, pool size 5 with overflow 5, `pool_timeout` 2 s, connect timeout 3 s, API `statement_timeout` 5 s, collector 30 s, `lock_timeout` 2 s; the engine is built per app in the lifespan and disposed at shutdown (no module state, Phase 2 D-23).
  - Neon's pooled endpoint (`prepare_threshold=None`, migrations over the direct URL) is a Phase 6 item. Postgres 17 in CI; migrations must also run on 16 (the container) so no 17-only feature is used. — **Reversibility:** costly — every repository and test touches it.
- **D-20:** Schema outline (the exact DDL is Alembic's; this is the shape to plan against). Types are `timestamptz` throughout; enums are `text` plus `CHECK`.
  - `accounts(id, pseudonym UNIQUE, first_observed_at)`.
  - `claims(id, kind CHECK in social_link/news_headline, text NULL, text_hash, url_canonical NULL, link_domain NULL, source_name NULL, published_at NULL, lang NULL, query_id NULL, created_at)`, unique on `(kind, text_hash, coalesce(url_canonical, ''))`. Social roots and news headlines share this table so Phase 4 can score both.
  - `cascades(id, root_uri UNIQUE, claim_id NULL, tracking_ends_at, first_event_at, last_event_at, event_count, unique_accounts, reply_count, quote_count, repost_count, max_depth, orphan_count, disconnected_count, deleted_count, order_violation_count, events_1h, overflow_count, root_deleted_at NULL, enrich_dirty, rolled_up_at, rollup_version)`.
  - `events(id, uri UNIQUE, edge_kind, inferred, parent_uri NULL, reply_root_uri NULL, quoted_uri NULL, parent_event_id NULL, parent_state, cascade_id NULL, depth NULL, account_id NULL, observed_at, occurred_at NULL, text NULL, text_purged_at NULL, claim_id NULL, lang NULL, deleted_at NULL)` with the checks of D-12, `text` only on roots, and `parent_event_id <> id`. Indexes are kept few because the budget is small: the unique `uri`, `(cascade_id, observed_at, uri)`, and a partial index on orphans.
  - `ingest_checkpoints(source PK, dialect, host, cursor_time_us, cursor_seq NULL, updated_at)` and `ingest_runs(id, source, dialect, host, mode, started_at, finished_at, status, resume_from_time_us, resume_gap, last_time_us, counters jsonb, error_class)`.
  - `news_queries(id, slug UNIQUE, query, last_attempt_at, last_success_at, last_status)` and `news_fetches(id, query_id, called_at, outcome)` (the quota ledger, D-26).
  - Not created in this phase: `claim_scores`, `model_versions`, `cascade_features`, simulation tables. Observed data and synthetic data never share tables, and no table has a `synthetic` column because nothing synthetic is stored.
  — **Reversibility:** costly — a migration to change; the spike gate exists to avoid doing it twice.
- **D-21:** Resource bounds for a free-tier managed Postgres (about 0.5 GB; STACK.md quotes 1 GB for Neon free, the smaller figure wins). Design budget: the application's data and indexes stay under 300 MB (`DB_BUDGET_BYTES`), stored events under 300,000 (`STORE_MAX_EVENTS_TOTAL`, to be recomputed from the S-8 bytes-per-row), 5,000 events per cascade, 500 active roots, text only on roots, few indexes, no `jsonb` payload copies of frames. The collector checks `pg_total_relation_size` at the start and every 50 batches and stops with status `capacity_reached` at 80% of the budget; the purge job and retention (OD-1) keep the steady state under it; batches write 500 rows or 2 s, whichever first, in one multi-row statement. Connection use: one for the collector, at most 10 for the API. Neon's autosuspend and monthly compute allowance are Phase 6 concerns, but a collector run is short on purpose (D-10).

### Enrich
- **D-22:** Enrich is a pure Python resolver with SQL used to select work and write results back. For each cascade flagged `enrich_dirty` it loads that cascade's rows (at most the per-cascade cap), runs a pure function `resolve_cascade(rows) -> ResolvedCascade` that sets `parent_event_id`, `parent_state`, `depth`, the rollup counters and the order violations, and writes the result in one transaction per cascade so readers see a consistent cascade.
  - Why Python and not set-based SQL: it reuses and extends the Phase 2 pure functions (`order_events`, depth and cycle detection in `core/cascade.py`), it is property-testable without a database, its sort keys are explicit, and cascade sizes are capped; SQL stays for the cheap work selection (`enrich_dirty`) and bulk write-back.
  - Determinism contract: the output is a pure function of the set of stored rows keyed by natural key (`uri`), independent of insertion order, of surrogate `id` values and of the wall clock (`events_1h` and `rolled_up_at` take an explicit `as_of`; `order_events` ties break on the URI key).
  - Enrich never raises on data: duplicate or inconsistent rows, cycles and self-parents are quarantined as `invalid` and counted, an unresolvable event stays an orphan.
  - The acceptance property test has two layers: Hypothesis over the pure resolver (random forests with orphans, tombstones, reposts and duplicate deliveries, shuffled, compared as a canonical projection keyed by URI) and a bounded-example database test that inserts permutations of the same rows, runs enrich, and compares the same projection from the tables. The resolver also asserts the Phase 2 invariants on the output (ordered by `observed_at`, a child never earlier than its parent except as counted violations). The pure resolver and rollup live in `enrich/` and import nothing from `db/`, `api/` or `ingest/`.

### `/v1` read API
- **D-23:** Shape. Routes are registered through `V1_REGISTRARS` on the `LimitedRouter` (rate limited by construction, `RATE_LIMIT_V1`), read-only, with typed response models and `extra="forbid"`:
  - `GET /v1/cascades` — the list; keyset pagination (opaque cursor encoding the sort key and id, malformed cursor is a 422), `limit` default 25, maximum 100, `sort` in `recent` (`last_event_at`) or `velocity` (`events_1h`), filters `claim_id`, `min_events`, `has_orphans`, and `include_deleted_roots` (default false). The Phase 5 "risk x velocity" ranking is not computed here (no risk exists yet).
  - `GET /v1/cascades/{cascade_id}` — summary and stats, including `observed_since`, orphan and disconnected counts, counts per edge kind, the number of ingest gaps overlapping its life, and the claim.
  - `GET /v1/cascades/{cascade_id}/tree` — nodes and edges, capped at 5,000 nodes in `order_events` order (a prefix of that order is closed under ancestors, so a capped tree stays connected), with `truncated`, `shown` and `total` so the UI can say "showing N of M"; each node has its short account pseudonym, `edge_kind`, `inferred`, `parent_state`, `observed_at`, `depth` and a deleted flag; detached subtrees are returned as separate roots; an optional `kinds` filter lets the UI leave reposts out.
  - `GET /v1/cascades/{cascade_id}/timeline` — bucketed counts per edge kind with a bucket size chosen from the cascade's span and bounded to at most 500 buckets.
  - `GET /v1/stats` — global counts, per-kind totals, orphan totals, `observed_since`, ingest lag, last run status, gap count, `source_mode`.
  - `GET /v1/news` — stored news context (D-26).
  Handlers are sync `def`, use short-lived sessions through a dependency, set per-statement timeouts, and reuse the Phase 2 TTL-cache pattern (per app, lock-guarded, successes only, errors never cached, short TTLs, keyed on the data version so a cache never outlives a data change by more than its TTL). A `/v1` operation declares problem+json responses 422, 429, 500 and 503 only (no 502: the API never calls an upstream); a test walks OpenAPI to prove it. API descriptions carry the caption "Observed subset of public Bluesky posts, as of the stated time" and "flag for human review" wording, and pass the copy guard. — **Reversibility:** costly — the Phase 5 client is generated from it.
- **D-24:** Provenance and completeness. Every `/v1` success body has `meta`: `synthetic: false` (typed `Literal[False]`), `data_as_of` (the stream time of the last committed checkpoint, null only when nothing was ever ingested), `source_status` in `ok`, `empty`, `stale` (stale when `data_as_of` is older than `INGEST_STALE_AFTER_SECONDS`, default 21,600), `source_mode` in `live`, `replay`, plus `observed_since` where meaningful. A cascade's `observed_since` is its root's `observed_at`: the cascade exists only from the moment its root was seen, and the API never implies completeness (orphan, disconnected, overflow and gap counts travel with it). Errors keep the Phase 1 problem body. A Bluesky outage surfaces as stale data with a failed run recorded, not as an API 502, because the API never calls Bluesky.
- **D-25:** Errors and readiness. A database connection failure, pool exhaustion or `OperationalError` maps to 503 problem+json with `Retry-After` (code `database_unavailable`, `upstream: "database"`, `source_status: "database_unavailable"`); a statement timeout maps to 503 `database_timeout` with `Retry-After`; any other database error is a generic 500 `internal_error` through the existing catch-all (the database answered, so it is our bug, never a 502) with no SQL, parameter or URL in the body or log; a process without `DATABASE_URL` (only possible under `DEMO_MODE`, D-27) raises the existing `ConfigurationError`, which renders the existing 500 `not_configured`. A `database` readiness check is registered through `register_default_checks`: it runs `SELECT 1` and compares `alembic_version` with the head revision read from the packaged script directory, failing closed on any difference, and it is a sync callable inside the registry's 2 s timeout. The new problem declarations reuse `_problem_declaration` and the `Retry-After` header schema. The proof (SC5) stops the test database or points the app at a closed port and asserts the 503 body, the header, the log contents (no URL) and `/ready` 503 naming only `database`.

### News context and the legacy `/analyze`
- **D-26:** News context (DATA-06). A scheduled-job entrypoint `python -m truthtrail.jobs news` refreshes a bounded query set and stores the result; the API only reads it. The query set is a committed file (`src/truthtrail/data/news_topics.toml`, parsed with `tomllib`) of at most 20 topics, each a subject or event phrase, never a named individual (the prototype's hardcoded-names defect), reviewed by the owner in the plan summary.
  - Quota: GNews' free plan is about 100 requests a day (LOW confidence until verified); the ledger table `news_fetches` records every call before it is made, a moving 24-hour count must be under `NEWS_DAILY_BUDGET` (default 60, leaving room for retries) or the run stops, each topic is refreshed at most every `NEWS_MIN_REFRESH_HOURS` (default 12) and a run makes at most `NEWS_MAX_CALLS_PER_RUN` calls (default 10).
  - The process-wide in-memory `UpstreamBudget` of Phase 2 is meaningless for a batch job and is replaced by this database ledger.
  - The job reuses `services/gnews.py` unchanged in behaviour (key only in the `X-Api-Key` header, timeouts, bounded retries with the 8 s budget, typed `UpstreamError`), adds a `max_results` parameter (default the plan's verified free-plan maximum; Phase 1 asked for one article), stops the whole run on a 503 `UpstreamError` (unreachable or throttling, including a provider 429) and records a 502 `UpstreamError` (a bad answer, including a rejected key) as a failed topic with a non-zero exit.
  - Results are stored as `claims(kind='news_headline')`: headline, canonical URL, source name, `published_at`, `fetched_at`; no article body, no risk label (the keyword baseline is baseline 0, not a product score), retained `RETENTION_NEWS_DAYS` (default 30).
  - `GET /v1/news` serves stored headlines by topic with the `/v1` provenance (`data_as_of` is the topic's last successful fetch). The job is the only code path that can reach the news API; `GNEWS_API_KEY` is read by it alone.
  - Proof for the roadmap's "no request handler can call the news API": (1) a static import-graph test shows nothing under `truthtrail.api` reaches `truthtrail.services.gnews`, `requests` or `websockets`, with a negative control; (2) a dynamic sweep calls every GET route of `create_app()` with representative parameters while `fetch_news` is patched to fail the test and the `responses` mock rejects any GNews URL; (3) the app starts and every route answers with `GNEWS_API_KEY` unset. The first live job run needs the owner's key and machine (a UAT item; the container never calls GNews).
- **D-27:** The fate of the legacy query-driven `/analyze`: retire it.
  - Options considered: **retire** (recommended), **reduce** (keep the path, serve only stored topics from the database), **keep behind a flag** (rejected: it keeps a user-controlled path to the news API, which DATA-06 forbids). Reduce would leave two overlapping surfaces (`/analyze` and `/v1/news`) with a confusing contract, a keyword `risk` label in the public API that contradicts "baseline 0 only", and a closed topic set masquerading as a search box. The primary UX is already the triage feed.
  - Consequences, all deliberate and done in the last plan of the phase, after `/v1/news` exists so the repository never lacks a working read surface: remove `GET /analyze`, `api/analyze.py`, `api/cache.py` and the API-side `UpstreamBudget`; a request to `/analyze` then gets the generic 404 problem+json; update the golden OpenAPI snapshot deliberately (`tests/golden/openapi_phase1.json` is replaced by a Phase 3 golden with the same declared-diff discipline); retire the `/analyze` contract, cache, rate-limit and retry tests with a written replacement map (as plan 01-05 did), moving the retry and timeout tests to the news job; keep `legacy/`, the synthetic demo fixture, its generator, the cascade-gate test and the 10 strict-xfail characterization tests untouched (they pin prototype defects and are not on the live path; `schemas.AnalyzeResponse` and friends may stay as fixture-schema types until the Phase 6 cleanup); keep `services/keyword_baseline.py` for Phase 4's baseline 0.
  - `DEMO_MODE` keeps its name and its fail-closed guarantee (it can never cause unlabelled synthetic data to be served) but its only effect becomes "start without `DATABASE_URL`, register no database check, and answer `/v1` data routes with `not_configured`"; this keeps the container smoke test valid, and renaming or removing the variable is deferred to Phase 6 when the README and compose are rewritten.
  - Configuration changes with it: `GNEWS_API_KEY` is required only by the news job, `DATABASE_URL` is required at startup (fail-fast, exit code 3, same pattern as Phase 2) unless `DEMO_MODE=true`, `PSEUDONYM_SECRET` is required by ingest and erase commands only; `Settings` stops requiring the news key globally and each entrypoint validates what it needs; the affected Phase 1 and 2 settings tests are retired with replacements. — **Reversibility:** costly — removes a public route, its tests and part of the settings contract.

### Testing, CI and ergonomics
- **D-28:** Testing against a real PostgreSQL. No mocked database for behaviour that depends on constraints, upserts, transactions or timeouts. Database tests carry `@pytest.mark.requires_db` (registered, `--strict-markers`).
  - Provisioning order of the session fixture: (1) `TEST_DATABASE_URL` if set (CI service container, a developer's server); tests never use `DATABASE_URL` (the autouse fixture already scrubs it, so a shell pointing at the real database cannot be touched), and they never run DDL in an existing database: they create `truthtrail_test_<random>`, run `alembic upgrade head` into it, and drop it at the end. (2) Otherwise a throwaway local cluster: find `initdb`, `pg_ctl` and `postgres` via `TRUTHTRAIL_PG_BIN`, the PATH, `/usr/lib/postgresql/*/bin` or the Windows `Program Files\PostgreSQL` directories; `initdb` into a temp directory with trust auth, listen on a unix socket (or a free loopback port on Windows), start with `pg_ctl -w`, stop and delete at the end. In this container PostgreSQL 16 is at `/usr/lib/postgresql/16/bin` and only `psql` is on the PATH; because Postgres refuses to run as root, when the effective uid is 0 the fixture runs `initdb` and `pg_ctl` as the existing `postgres` system user (uid 102) in a directory that user owns; the first database plan records the exact working invocation. (3) Otherwise the session fails loudly: one clear error per session, not per test, naming the remedies.
  - A database test never skips silently: the default `uv run pytest` fails when no database can be provisioned, the documented fast loop is `pytest -m "not requires_db"`, CI and the verification commands set `REQUIRE_DB=1` and additionally assert that the number of executed database tests is above a floor, so absence can never read as green. A deselect-by-default marker was rejected: a green run could hide untested database paths, the failure mode this phase forbids.
  - Autouse network guard: the `responses` mock covers `requests` only, so add a socket guard allowing loopback and unix sockets only; the live Jetstream is never touched in tests.
  - Property tests use the `ci` Hypothesis profile (200 examples, derandomized) for the pure resolver and a bounded number of examples for database layers. Subprocess tests pass `SYSTEMROOT` through.
  - The crash-resume proof (SC3) runs the CLI as a subprocess over the fixture with a small batch size and a per-batch delay, waits until a checkpoint row exists, kills the process, reruns to completion, and compares the final counts and projection with an uninterrupted run. A pinned golden of row counts after replaying the fixture is derived from the first verified run and explained in the test.
- **D-29:** CI. The `tests` job gains a Postgres 17 service container pinned by tag and digest (the Phase 2 digest-pin discipline, resolved at planning time) with a health check, and `TEST_DATABASE_URL` plus `REQUIRE_DB=1`; throwaway service credentials in the workflow are not secrets and are written so the credential backstop does not read them as such. A CI step also runs the real `alembic upgrade head`, `downgrade base`, `upgrade head` and `alembic check` against the service, in addition to the pytest migration test. The coverage gate only ratchets upward; new modules must be covered. The `docker` job keeps its `DEMO_MODE=true` smoke and gains a check that the migration package is importable in the image. The Dockerfile needs no new copy rule because migrations live under `src/`; `tests/test_container_files.py` and `tests/test_ci_workflow.py` are extended, not weakened. `pip-audit` covers the new dependencies. No scheduled workflow is added in this phase (it would need secrets and a database that exist only in Phase 6); the commands are scheduling-ready.
- **D-30:** Windows ergonomics for the owner (PowerShell, Python 3.13, uv, Windows 11). Everything runs with `uv sync` and `uv run`: `psycopg[binary]` and `websockets` ship Windows wheels; no `uvloop`, no `fork`, no `loop.add_signal_handler`; paths via `pathlib`, files opened with explicit encoding and LF (`.gitattributes` already enforces it); new developer helpers are Python scripts, not bash. The spike tool runs as `uv run python scripts/bluesky_spike.py record --seconds 300 --out spike-output` and prints a short summary the owner can paste. For tests the README documents three routes: `winget install PostgreSQL.PostgreSQL.17` and set `$env:TEST_DATABASE_URL` (a PowerShell example with a `<password>` placeholder), or set `TRUTHTRAIL_PG_BIN` to a local install so the fixture builds a throwaway cluster, or run `uv run pytest -m "not requires_db"` and rely on CI for the database layers. Corporate proxy and certificates use `HTTPS_PROXY` and `SSL_CERT_FILE`; TLS verification is never disabled.
- **D-31:** Layout, import rules and security hygiene. New packages beside the Phase 2 ones: `db/` (models, session and URL handling, `migrations/`, repositories), `ingest/` (dialects, normaliser, pseudonymiser, selection, writer, run loop, CLI), `enrich/` (pure resolver, rollup, apply, purge), `jobs/` (news job and CLI), `schemas/v1.py`, `core/policy.py`. `core/cascade.py` is extended additively and `core` imports no higher layer. An AST import-rule test (extending Phase 2's `test_no_legacy_imports`, with negative controls) enforces: `api` never imports `ingest`, `jobs`, `services.gnews`, `requests` or `websockets`; `ingest`, `enrich` and `jobs` never import `api`; `enrich` pure modules import nothing from `db`; the API process never imports the pseudonymiser. Security checks for the Phase 3 gate: parameterised SQL only; no URL from a post is ever fetched; bounded response sizes (tree 5,000 nodes, pages <= 100, timeline <= 500 buckets) and statement timeouts; a WebSocket frame size bound; no personal data, SQL, URL or secret in logs or bodies (tests with a marker value, as in Phase 2); `DATABASE_URL`, `PSEUDONYM_SECRET` and `COLLECTOR_CONTACT` handled as secrets or operator data; the `.env.example` list, the credential-test name set and the conftest scrub list are extended for every new variable; user-generated text, when shown, is plain text only (Phase 5 renders it as text).

## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets
- `core/cascade.py` (`order_events`, `validate_cascade`, `_depths` cycle detection, `CascadeEvent`): the ordering and invariant kernel for the enrich resolver; ties break on the event id, so pass the URI key as `event_id`.

- `api/v1.py` (`V1_REGISTRARS`, `build_v1_router`) and `api/limits.py` (`LimitedRouter`): every `/v1` route is rate limited by construction; handlers must declare `request: Request`.
- `core/errors.py` and `api/problems.py`: one problem+json builder, `Retry-After` handling, `_problem_declaration` and the OpenAPI post-processing that keeps only `application/problem+json`.
- `api/cache.py` (`AnalyzeCache`, single-flight TTL cache): a pattern to generalise for `/v1` reads.
- `services/gnews.py`: key in `X-Api-Key` only, no redirects, bounded retries, typed errors; reused by the news job.

- `scripts/gitleaks_*.sh`, `scripts/copy_guard.py`, the credential backstop test: guards the new fixture and docs must pass.

### Established Patterns
- Characterization first and strict xfails; any retirement of tests is deliberate with a replacement map, never silent.
- One error shape with provenance members on every error path; success bodies carry provenance.
- Per-app state built in `create_app()`; no module-level mutable state; construction-time values from `BootSettings`; fail-fast startup in the lifespan (exit code 3).
- Exact-pinned dependencies with a hashed `uv.lock`, SHA-pinned actions, digest-pinned images, `persist-credentials: false`.
- Secrets only via the environment; tests use obviously fake values; scans print counts, never matched text.
- Commits end with the two attribution trailers; key-material scan before every commit; push after each plan.
